Every screen, in context

Each shot is the finished app on an iPhone 16 Pro Max, running a frozen demo book dated 20 November 2026. Numbers are virtual. Nothing here is a live brokerage account.

Home — the book in one number

Home is the first look at the account: net liquidation value, the day change, and the QQQ smile for a chosen move. The gold and blue lines compare constant volatility with a realized-vol path, so you can see how the book behaves if the index moves three percent today.

This is the whole book as it stands — no virtual legs, no horizon filter. It is the picture you glance at before you start changing anything.

Analysis, Now — the smile of the full book

Analysis keeps the same net liquidation, then lets you choose a horizon. Now (Today) keeps every option still in the book, including the 0DTE hedges that expire on the session you are looking at. The caption reads “whole book,” and the smile stays that familiar U-shape.

This is the baseline: what the book looks like if today still counts.

Analysis, Tomorrow — after 0DTE comes off

Tomorrow drops every option that expires before the next session. On this demo book that is the 20 November 0DTE, 220 contracts. The caption switches to “without options 20.11,” and the smile changes shape: without those hedges, an up-move in QQQ no longer pays the same way.

That is the point of the horizon. You see the book you will actually wake up with, not the one that still includes options that will be gone at the close.

Virtual leg — try a trade, send nothing

The virtual leg is calculation only. You pick an expiry, a call or a put, buy or sell, a strike, and a quantity. The market price and implied vol come from the demo chain. Until quantity is above zero, the leg stays off the chart — you can set it up without moving the smile.

When you add it, Analysis redraws the book as if the trade were already on. Nothing is sent to a broker.

Legs and the stepper — change size in place

Under the smile, the book is listed by expiry. Here the 20 November 0DTE hedges sit above the January 2027 core. Each row has a quantity stepper, Close, and undo, so you can try a smaller or larger size and see the smile move before you commit to anything in the real world.

Reset all puts the book back the way the demo opened it.

Vol — is the market cheap or dear?

Vol answers a different question from the smile. “Vol now” says, in plain language, whether implied vol is below or above what QQQ usually does, and what VXN and VIX are doing on the day. The ATM table and the straddles turn that into numbers: a mid, an expected move, and a range, tenor by tenor.

The straddles are an expected move, not a trade idea.

Live Activity settings — Compact or Two lines

From Settings you choose how Convexa sits on the Lock Screen. Compact is a single strip: net liquidation, the day change, and QQQ. Two lines adds VIX and VXN underneath. Live stays off in the demo, so the activity shows the frozen book rather than a live feed.

Lock Screen — the book while the phone is asleep

Compact is for a glance. Two lines is for when you also want the tape: QQQ, VIX, and VXN next to the day change. Both cards are the official demo Live Activity, rendered at the 398 pt Lock Screen width of iPhone Pro Max. The figures are the same demo book you see in the app.

Dynamic Island — the same book, in the pill

The compact Island is a short pill: net liquidation as $64.6K and the day as a percent. Expand it and you get the Two lines layout — the full NLV, the dollar change, and QQQ, VIX, and VXN. It is meant to stay readable while you are in another app.

Home Screen widgets — the book on the desktop

The small widget holds net liquidation, the day change, and a “now” marker. The medium widget adds QQQ and a Flat vol / Hedged sketch. Both use the same demo book as the rest of this page — not a sample account with made-up hundreds of thousands.

Premarket — the book before the cash open

Before the cash session, the same Two lines card can carry a premarket caption. The demo book is unchanged; the caption tells you the session, so you do not read a pre-open mark as a regular-hours last. On this showcase it is a composed Lock Screen mock of that state.

How it’s calculated

The app prices the book; it does not place orders. The rules below are the ones behind every smile on this page.

01

Black-Scholes, leg by leg

Each option is valued with Black-Scholes (Black–Scholes–Merton, with a dividend yield). The smile you see is the sum of those legs at a given QQQ move, not a single fitted curve on the index.

02

Tomorrow without 0DTE

The Tomorrow horizon drops every option that expires before the next session. On this demo book that is the 20 November 0DTE. Today keeps those legs; Tomorrow shows the book you have once they are gone.

03

IBKR, every minute

In live use, marks and net liquidation come from Interactive Brokers, refreshed about once a minute through Convexa’s feed. This page uses a frozen demo snapshot instead, so the figures never change while you read.

04

Premarket is labelled

Before the cash open, the Lock Screen and the app can show a premarket state. The book is still marked; the caption is there so a pre-open price is not mistaken for a regular-hours close.
